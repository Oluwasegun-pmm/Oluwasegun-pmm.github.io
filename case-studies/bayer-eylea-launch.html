Case Study 05 · Regulated Pharma GTM · Bayer, March 2019 — August 2020

Eylea launch — regulated ophthalmology GTM in Nigeria

Designed and ran the full go-to-market for Eylea (aflibercept), Bayer's anti-VEGF ophthalmology therapy, in a market with no existing category behavior, sparse specialist coverage, and mandatory regulatory and medical review at every commercial touchpoint.

01The problem

Eylea was entering a market where anti-VEGF intravitreal therapy for retinal disease was barely an established category — most ophthalmologists were still managing patients with older, less effective standards of care, and patients themselves had no framework for understanding why a newer, costlier injection therapy was worth adopting. Every piece of messaging, every physician education program, and every patient-facing material had to clear medical, legal, and regulatory review before it could reach the field — the kind of approval loop that kills GTM velocity if you don't design for it upfront.

02The insight

The launch wouldn't be won on message polish — it would be won on physician conviction. Ophthalmologists needed to see peer-level clinical evidence and hands-on case support before they'd change a standard of care they'd used for years, and patients needed a referring physician who was already convinced, not a brochure. The GTM had to be built around building specialist confidence first, category awareness second.

03Decisions I made

  • Built the launch plan and messaging architecture in parallel with the medical/legal/regulatory review track from day one, instead of writing materials and then discovering what compliance would allow — this is what protected launch velocity in a market where every asset needed sign-off.
  • Prioritized specialist-level clinical engagement (case-based education, peer discussion formats) over broad awareness spend, on the thesis that ophthalmologist conviction was the actual conversion lever.
  • Coordinated medical, commercial, and regulatory stakeholders through every launch milestone so approvals didn't become the bottleneck once field execution started.
  • Owned the full product lifecycle — not just launch-day messaging — so post-launch adoption signal could feed back into physician engagement priorities in real time.

04Outcome

  • Adoption reached 300%+ of the original 6-month projection.
  • Revenue scaled to 7 figures within 12 months of launch.
  • Established a repeatable model for launching specialty therapies into markets with underdeveloped category behavior — used as a reference for subsequent specialty launches.

05Learnings

  • Compliance review is a launch-timeline input, not a launch-timeline risk. Build it into the plan from day one and it stops being the thing that blows deadlines.
  • In categories with no existing behavior, you're not marketing a product — you're building physician conviction first. Everything else follows from that.
  • The discipline is domain-agnostic. The same instinct — find the actual gatekeeper, build the artifact that unblocks them — is what later shaped how I approached AI evaluation frameworks at Microsoft.
Note This predates my Microsoft tenure and is included to show the GTM discipline travels across regulated domains — pharma and enterprise AI — not just within one company's distribution.
