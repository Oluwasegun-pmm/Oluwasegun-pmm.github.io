AI & Agentic Systems for Core AI, Data & Cloud-Native Platforms

01The problem

Enterprise customers across Core AI, Data, and Cloud-Native platforms want generative and agentic AI, but every pilot stalled at the same three gates: we couldn't prove output reliability to risk & compliance, we couldn't audit what the agent did and why, and we couldn't trace model or workflow changes back to a decision. Without those, AI workloads didn't leave the sandbox — no matter how impressive the demo.

The actual blocker wasn't the economic buyer. It was the risk & compliance reviewer sitting between a champion and a signature — they had no artifact to stamp, so pilots stalled in review indefinitely.

02The insight

The blocker isn't the model, it's the evaluation artifact. Enterprise reviewers don't need more marketing — they need a documented acceptance framework they can defend internally. If the PMM ships that framework as part of the product, adoption unlocks.

03Decisions I made

  • Formalized AI evaluation frameworks — quality, accuracy, drift, and output monitoring requirements — as product artifacts versioned alongside the roadmap, written so a compliance reviewer, not just an engineer, could sign off on them.
  • Rewrote field messaging from capability-first to defensibility-first: replaced "here's what the model can do" decks with a one-page evaluation summary and audit-trail artifact that sales could hand directly to a customer's risk team.
  • Defined acceptance criteria at the sprint level for AI and agentic features so engineering ships against measurable quality bars, not subjective sign-off.
  • Positioned agentic workloads on platforms with explicit observability and policy hooks (AKS, Azure AI) — rejecting deployment paths that broke auditability.

04Outcome

  • AI evaluation frameworks and monitoring requirements formally documented and adopted by 6+ internal teams, becoming a reusable pattern beyond Copilot.
  • The evaluation-summary artifact became a standard sales enablement asset field teams attached directly to compliance review packets — shifting the conversation from "trust us" to "here's what to check."
  • Cross-functional alignment across product, engineering, field, legal, and partners without direct authority, on a unified roadmap for trustworthy AI.
  • Deals that previously stalled in compliance review now have a documented path through it — the artifact is the default attachment field teams use to move a pilot past the risk gate.

05Learnings

  • The buyer isn't always the blocker. In regulated AI, the compliance reviewer decides whether the buyer's yes survives contact with risk — write for them.
  • Write the evaluation framework before the roadmap. In trust-critical domains it is the roadmap — everything else is scaffolding.
  • Auditability is a product feature, not a post-sale deliverable. Scope it in acceptance criteria.
  • Trust compounds. The first published benchmark is the expensive one; every subsequent feature inherits its defensibility.
